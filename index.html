Control that learns from data, and can still be trusted.

I study how learning-based methods can be given the guarantees that control theory provides. My work joins robust and predictive control with data-driven design, reinforcement learning, generative AI, large language models and expert systems, and applies them to power and renewable energy, biological and biomedical systems, and autonomous robots.

Methods

Theory and learning in one toolbox

Classical control and modern AI are treated as parts of one design problem, chosen for what can be guaranteed as well as for what performs best.

All research themes →

  • Control theory

    Robust, optimal and predictive control: H∞ and LMI-based design, tube and event-triggered MPC, sliding-mode and adaptive methods for uncertain, delayed and constrained systems.

  • Data-driven control

    Controllers designed directly from measured trajectories, without first identifying a model: data-enabled predictive control (DeePC) and data-driven H∞ design with stability and constraint guarantees.

  • Reinforcement learning

    Deep reinforcement learning for long-horizon decisions, where reward arrives late and credit is hard to assign, and learning-guided optimisation for large design problems.

  • Generative AI and large language models

    Where generative AI and LLMs add value in engineering systems (forecasting, design, operation and decision support), and how far their outputs can be relied on.

  • Expert systems

    Knowledge-based and probabilistic reasoning, from fuzzy inference to Bayesian networks, for diagnosis, reliability assessment and supervisory decisions that stay explainable.

The common thread

Robust and trustworthy by design

A method earns its place in a wind turbine, a treatment plan or a robot working beside people only if it keeps behaving well when the model is wrong, the data are noisy or a component fails. Four questions run through every project.

  • Robustness

    Does performance hold under model uncertainty, disturbances, delays and actuator limits?

  • Safety

    Are state and input constraints respected at every step, even when the controller is built from data alone?

  • Resilience

    Does the system detect and ride through sensor faults and cyber-attacks?

  • Deployability

    Is it data-efficient and light enough to run in real time on real hardware?

Background

Career in brief

I received my BSc, MSc and PhD degrees in Electrical Engineering from Yıldız Technical University (YTU), Istanbul, in 1997, 1999 and 2002. At YTU I was Assistant Professor (2004–2010), Associate Professor (2010–2015) and Full Professor (2015–2017). I joined Glasgow Caledonian University in 2017 and have been a Reader since 2021.

I was Principal Investigator of ROBMAN, an EPSRC project on robust robotic manipulation underwater with Heriot-Watt University and Subsea 7, and my research has attracted more than £1.4 million in competitive and industrial funding. I have supervised six PhD students to completion and examine doctoral theses in the UK and abroad.
